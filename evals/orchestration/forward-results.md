## Case A — validation plan completed; idea unvalidated

The marketplace is an unvalidated hypothesis. No interviews, market size, demand, or willingness to pay is established. Proposed initial segment: homeowners needing one repair category in one locality; buyer is the homeowner; provider is the repair professional. The hypothesized painful job is finding a trustworthy available provider and confirming scope, price and time. Existing alternatives to investigate include referrals, directories and direct calls.

Value hypothesis: people with a recent repair need will choose assisted booking over their current method, while enough providers accept the resulting jobs at economics that cover acquisition and coordination. Desirability risk is insufficient pain or trust; feasibility is reliable availability and fulfilment; viability is fees versus acquisition/support costs; distribution is economically reaching both sides in the same locality.

Compare three options: current referrals/direct booking have familiar trust but require coordination; a manually operated concierge tests matching with low build cost but high operator effort; a marketplace could automate matching but adds supply density and software risk. This comparison does not establish which wins.

Proposed plan only, for future separately authorized execution:
1. Days 1–7: interview 12 recent repair buyers and 8 providers in one category/locality about actual past jobs, delays, alternatives, fees and failed bookings. Proposed gate: at least 7 buyers report a concrete unresolved coordination problem and 4 providers can offer usable availability at a fee model to be tested. Hypothetical enthusiasm alone does not count.
2. Days 8–21, only if the first gate passes: offer a manual concierge to 10 qualified people with real repair needs, using existing communication tools. Proposed gate: at least 5 request a booking, at least 4 jobs complete within the agreed service window, and the accepted fee covers observed variable coordination/support costs. Record acquisition effort and cost; do not assume free acquisition will scale.
3. Proceed to a tightly scoped pilot only if both gates pass. Pivot the segment or problem if a clear alternative pain emerges. Stop this category/locality proposition if meaningful pain is absent or reliable fulfilment cannot be achieved at workable economics. These thresholds are proposed decisions, not market benchmarks or statistical proof.

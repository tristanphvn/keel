# Discover product value

1. Identify the target user, buyer, painful job, current alternative and constraints. Treat founder beliefs as hypotheses until supported.
2. Form a falsifiable value hypothesis and distinguish desirability, feasibility, viability and distribution risks. Label market numbers and willingness-to-pay claims by evidence; do not invent interviews or demand.
3. Compare the current alternative, a smaller solution and the proposed product. Describe tradeoffs without replacing the user's goal.
4. Select the riskiest assumption and a low-cost experiment with an observable metric, decision threshold and time bound. State what outcome would invalidate the idea. Proposed thresholds are decisions, not market facts.
5. Define the smallest MVP that tests value, explicit exclusions and a proceed/pivot/stop decision conditional on evidence. Hand missing facts to Research, and an accepted scope to Planning; do not begin implementation from an unapproved hypothesis.

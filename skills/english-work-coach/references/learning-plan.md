# 12-week English coaching plan

## Learner and constraints

Read the learner-owned record for identity, preferred language, goals, daily budget, self-reported ability, and assessment evidence. If missing, collect only what is needed, one question at a time. Do not treat the syllabus as evidence of a learner's background or level. Keep personal profile details out of these canonical teaching instructions.

This is an adaptable 12-week plan for everyday and work communication. The proposed daily budget is 20–30 minutes; confirm it against the learner's stated availability. When coaching is requested, default to Strict English subject to the exceptions in SKILL.md. Honor opt-out in any language and adjust difficulty to observed performance.

12 weeks at 20–30 minutes daily gives 28–42 scheduled hours. Aim for measurable improvement on familiar tasks, not a guaranteed CEFR jump or unrestricted fluency.

## Daily routine

Standard 25 minutes: 3 retrieval of previous phrases; 7 listen to a short accessible clip and check meaning; 10 speak/role-play with selective feedback and retry; 5 write a useful prompt or recap and schedule review.
20 minutes: 3/5/8/4. 30 minutes: 3/8/13/6.
If audio is unavailable, label the input/output as reading/writing, and offer a learner-operated listening and recording task; do not silently substitute and score it as listening/speaking.

Weekly rhythm: days 1–4 new phrases plus retrieval; day 5 transfer to a new scenario; day 6 conversation and repair of recurring errors; day 7 light review and weekly check. All fit the daily budget. If a day is missed, resume with a short review; no doubled workload.

## Weekly syllabus

| Week | Communicative task | Language focus | Observable output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce yourself and your work; baseline | be, present simple, basic questions | 45–60 second introduction or labelled written equivalent |
| 2 | Discuss routine, hobbies, and plans | frequency, time, present continuous | 6-turn everyday exchange |
| 3 | Ask for help and clarification | could, can, question order, repair phrases | ask 3 useful follow-up questions |
| 4 | Combine everyday topics; checkpoint | review and connectors | 2-minute familiar-topic talk plus 3 follow-ups |
| 5 | Give a work update | past/present/future contrast | yesterday/today/blocker update |
| 6 | Explain a bug and its impact | sequence, expected vs actual | simple bug explanation and clarification exchange |
| 7 | Clarify requirements and constraints | if, need, should, must | ask before assuming; summarize agreement |
| 8 | Explain a choice; checkpoint | because, however, comparisons | 3–5 minute supported work conversation |
| 9 | Tell a project story | past events, role, result | short truthful project narrative |
| 10 | Handle interviews | experience, strengths, concrete examples | answer 3 common interview questions |
| 11 | Handle small talk and meetings | polite disagreement, turn-taking, repair | unfamiliar role-play without a prepared script |
| 12 | Final checkpoint and next plan | consolidate individual gaps | target 5-minute familiar conversation and follow-ups |

Balance approximately 40% everyday topics and 60% work/career across the course; adapt to evidence. Start with simple language, not an assumption that every A2-labelled resource fits.

## Baseline, day 1 (25 minutes)

3 minutes: introduce goal and check audio capability.
5 minutes: learner introduction and follow-up, no model answer first.
5 minutes: short genuine audio comprehension if accessible; otherwise mark listening unassessed.
5 minutes: learner writes a real generic work prompt in 3–5 sentences.
5 minutes: correct two priorities and ask for a retry.
2 minutes: record observed baseline and next step.

## Measurement

At baseline and weeks 4/8/12: introduction, explanation of a familiar work problem, 3 unprepared follow-ups, comparable short audio with 5 comprehension items where available, and a new transfer scenario. Use comparable difficulty, not memorized answers. Record replay count and support given. Target 4/5 listening items after at most two listens only when suitable material and baseline make it realistic.

Rubric 0–3 per observed dimension: task completion, comprehensibility, usable vocabulary, sentence control. 0 = cannot yet communicate the intended content; 1 = communicates fragments with substantial help; 2 = communicates main points with some support; 3 = independently communicates the intended points clearly. Report dimensions separately, not as a CEFR score. Mark missing evidence N/A. Record oral response pauses/repair only when audio supports this, not from chat latency. Track recurring errors and ability to retrieve phrases in a novel sentence after a week.

Use checkpoints to adjust: persistent difficulty → shorter input and fewer phrases; independent success in two novel tasks → add a follow-up or slightly harder audio. Never progress purely because the calendar advanced.

## Shared use

Use the same SKILL.md and references in both assistants; keep a single learner-owned progress record and avoid concurrent edits. Installation and discovery depend on the actual runtime; do not claim this ChatGPT install has installed anything on the learner's computer or in Claude. If a runtime cannot discover the skill, explicitly load its instructions and references in that session. Provide setup instructions only after inspecting or verifying that runtime's supported mechanism.
